Emphasize:
- In this textbook's terminology, a linear function of two variables has the form $f(x,y)=c+mx+ny$; state the constant and coordinate-direction slopes.
- A constant slope along one coordinate direction alone does not establish linearity in both variables.
- Numerical tests require consistent changes over the relevant table entries; a few matching values do not prove a function is linear everywhere.
- For a nonconstant linear function, contours are parallel lines, and equal changes in level give equally spaced contours in the plane.
- A level surface $F(x,y,z)=k$ is a subset of three-dimensional space; a family of such surfaces represents a three-variable function.
- The graph of $z=f(x,y)$ must assign exactly one $z$ to every $(x,y)$ in its domain. A level surface may fail this test or split into branches.
- Surface equations, axis directions, cross-sections, and domain restrictions must be checked before naming or sketching a shape.
